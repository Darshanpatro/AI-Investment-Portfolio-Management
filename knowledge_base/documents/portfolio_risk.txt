Title: Portfolio Risk

Portfolio risk refers to the possibility that the actual return on an
investment portfolio will differ from its expected return, including the
possibility of losing some or all of the invested capital. Risk in a
portfolio context is usually discussed in terms of several distinct types.

Concentration risk arises when a large portion of a portfolio is invested
in a single asset, sector, or asset type. If that concentrated position
performs poorly, it can have an outsized negative effect on the entire
portfolio. A commonly used rule of thumb is to flag concentration when a
single sector or asset exceeds a configured percentage of total portfolio
value, though the exact "safe" threshold varies depending on the investor's
risk tolerance and is not a universal fixed number.

Market risk (or systematic risk) is the risk that affects the overall
market or a large segment of it, driven by factors such as interest rate
changes, inflation, geopolitical events, or recessions. Diversification
cannot fully protect against market risk.

Liquidity risk is the risk that an investor will not be able to buy or sell
an asset quickly enough, at a fair price, when needed.

Risk is often measured using statistical tools such as standard deviation
(volatility) of returns, drawdown from a portfolio's peak value, and
anomaly detection on price movement patterns to flag unusual behavior that
may indicate elevated risk.

Understanding portfolio risk is important not because it predicts future
prices with certainty, but because it helps investors understand what
could happen under adverse conditions and make more informed decisions
about position sizing and diversification.
