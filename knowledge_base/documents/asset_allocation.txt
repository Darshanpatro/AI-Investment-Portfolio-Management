Title: Asset Allocation

Asset allocation is the process of dividing an investment portfolio among
different asset categories, such as stocks, bonds, cash, and alternative
investments like real estate or commodities. The allocation an investor
chooses is one of the most important decisions in portfolio management,
because different asset classes carry different levels of risk and return
potential, and they often respond differently to the same economic
conditions.

There is no single "correct" asset allocation. The right mix depends on
factors such as the investor's time horizon, financial goals, risk
tolerance, and current life stage. A young investor with a long time
horizon before retirement can typically afford to take on more risk by
allocating a larger share of their portfolio to equities (stocks), since
they have more time to recover from short-term market downturns. An
investor nearing retirement often shifts toward a larger allocation of
bonds and cash to preserve capital and reduce volatility.

Asset allocation percentage for a given asset is generally calculated as:
(value of the asset / total portfolio value) x 100.

Over time, as different assets grow at different rates, a portfolio's
actual allocation will drift away from its target allocation. This is why
investors periodically rebalance their portfolios, buying or selling assets
to bring the allocation back in line with their intended targets.

Asset allocation is closely related to, but distinct from, diversification.
Asset allocation is about deciding how much to put into each category,
while diversification is about spreading investments within and across
those categories to reduce concentrated risk.
