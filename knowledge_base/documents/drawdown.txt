Title: Drawdown

Drawdown is a measure of the decline in the value of a portfolio or asset
from a previous peak (its highest recorded value) to a subsequent trough
(a lower value), expressed as a percentage. It is one of the most widely
used measures of downside risk because it captures the real, experienced
loss an investor would have seen if they had bought at the peak.

The formula for drawdown at any given point in time is:
Drawdown = (Current Value - Previous Peak Value) / Previous Peak Value x 100

Because the current value is typically lower than the peak when a drawdown
is occurring, this value is usually negative or zero. A drawdown of 0%
means the portfolio is currently at its all-time high.

Maximum drawdown refers to the largest peak-to-trough decline observed
over a specified period. It is often used as a way to understand the
worst-case historical loss an investor would have experienced, and is
commonly used alongside average returns to judge whether a strategy's
returns are worth its historical risk.

Drawdowns are a normal part of investing; nearly all portfolios experience
some drawdown over time, even ones with strong long-term returns. What
matters to most investors is the magnitude and duration of drawdowns
relative to their own risk tolerance and time horizon, since a large
drawdown that takes years to recover from can be much more difficult to
endure than a shallow, short-lived one.
