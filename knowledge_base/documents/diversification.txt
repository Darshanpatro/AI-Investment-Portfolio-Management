Title: Diversification

Diversification is an investment strategy that involves spreading capital
across multiple assets, asset classes, sectors, or geographies so that the
performance of any single investment has a limited impact on the overall
portfolio. The core idea is that different assets do not always move in the
same direction at the same time. When one investment performs poorly,
another may perform well, which reduces the overall volatility of the
portfolio.

Diversification does not eliminate risk entirely. It primarily reduces
unsystematic risk, also called specific risk, which is the risk associated
with a single company or sector, such as a product recall, management
scandal, or sector-specific regulation change. It does not protect against
systematic risk, also called market risk, which affects nearly all
investments at the same time, such as a broad economic recession.

A well-diversified portfolio typically includes a mix of asset classes
(stocks, bonds, ETFs, cash, and sometimes commodities or real estate),
multiple sectors (technology, banking, energy, healthcare, and so on), and
sometimes multiple geographies (domestic and international markets).

A common warning sign of poor diversification is concentration risk, where
a large percentage of the portfolio is invested in a single stock or
sector. If one sector represents an unusually large share of a portfolio,
a downturn in that sector can significantly harm the investor's overall
returns.

Investors should periodically review their asset allocation and rebalance
their portfolio to maintain their desired level of diversification, since
some holdings will grow faster than others over time.
